# Issue B1.3 Solution — Compare Two Solutions

## 1. Architectural Pattern Applied & Rationale
- **Comparative Trade-off Analyzer / Component Contract Adapter (`app/engine/metrics.py`):**
  - `compare_two_solutions` computes directional deltas (**Solution B − Solution A**) and student-level allocation shifts, returning a dictionary matching `models.RunComparison` and `app/visualization/templates/compare.html`.

## 2. High-Level Logic & Data Flow Walkthrough
1. **Cohort Consistency Validation:**
   - Maps both solutions' assignments by `student_id` and verifies both solutions cover the exact same student cohort (`ValueError` if mismatched).
2. **Student-Level Reallocation Detection (`changed_assignments`):**
   - Identifies students whose `project_id` differs between Solution A and Solution B.
   - Uses penalty rank `max(all_ranks) + 1` when an assignment is unranked (`None`) to compute `rank_delta = compare_rank_b - compare_rank_a`.
   - Negative `rank_delta` increments `rank_improved_count` (moved to a better choice number, e.g., Choice #3 -> Choice #1); positive `rank_delta` increments `rank_worsened_count`.
3. **Aggregate Metric Deltas (`delta_metrics`):**
   - Computes `delta_mean_skill_fit`, `delta_mean_rank`, `delta_unranked_count`, and `delta_gini` rounded to 4 decimal places to avoid floating-point representation artefacts (such as `0.95 - 0.55 = 0.3999999999999999`).
4. **Human-Readable Trade-off Summary (`trade_off_summary`):**
   - Translates numerical deltas into plain-English statements describing skill-fit changes, average rank changes, and how many students improved or worsened their preference rank.

## 5. Pedagogical Checkpoint ("Check Your Understanding")
1. **Why does a negative `rank_delta` (`rank_b - rank_a < 0`) mean a student's outcome improved?**
   - Because preference ranks are 1-indexed where `1` is the 1st choice and `3` is the 3rd choice; moving from rank 3 (`rank_a = 3`) to rank 1 (`rank_b = 1`) yields `1 - 3 = -2` (2 ranks better).
2. **Why must deltas be rounded to 4 decimal places?**
   - IEEE 754 floating-point subtraction (e.g., `0.95 - 0.55`) produces `0.3999999999999999`, which would fail exact equality assertions (`== 0.4`) and clutter the UI.
